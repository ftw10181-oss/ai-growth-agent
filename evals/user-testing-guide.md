# Moderated User Test Guide — V0.1

## Research question

Does AI Growth Agent help growth practitioners reach useful, testable user hypotheses faster than their current starting process?

## Participants

Recruit five participants with recent responsibility for growth, product operations, international marketing, or early-stage product strategy.

Record only an anonymous participant code, role family, and experience band. Do not collect employer-confidential briefs or unnecessary personal data.

## Study design

Use a within-participant, counterbalanced comparison:

- Task A: work from one brief using the participant's normal starting process.
- Task B: work from a matched brief using AI Growth Agent.
- Alternate which task comes first across participants.

Use different but similarly difficult briefs to reduce learning effects. Do not compare outputs from the exact same brief in sequence.

## Participant task

> Review this product-growth brief. Produce a primary target-user hypothesis and at least three questions you would take into discovery interviews. Stop when you believe the material is usable by a growth team.

## Measures

### Behavioral

- Time to first usable target-user hypothesis
- Time to first usable research question
- Total task time
- Number of items the participant keeps without rewriting
- Number of unsupported claims identified by the moderator

### Quality

Apply `rubric.md` to both task outputs:

- Specificity
- Relevance
- Testability
- Internal consistency
- Unsupported-claim safety

### Perception

Ask participants to rate from 1 to 5:

- I understand how the output was derived.
- The output gives me a useful starting point.
- The assumptions are sufficiently visible.
- I would use this in a real early-stage growth task.

## Moderator script

1. Explain that the product generates hypotheses, not verified market research.
2. Ask the participant to think aloud without teaching them the interface.
3. Start timing when the brief becomes visible.
4. Mark the first target-user statement and research question the participant considers usable.
5. Do not help with content; only resolve interface blockers.
6. After both tasks, conduct the debrief below.

## Debrief questions

1. Which output changed how you framed the user problem, if any?
2. Which item felt most specific and useful?
3. Which item felt generic, incorrect, or repetitive?
4. What assumption would you validate first?
5. What would you need before trusting this in a real project?
6. Which part would you copy into your next workflow?
7. What would make you stop using the product?

## Reporting

Report the sample size and use medians for time measures. Show raw denominators for percentages, for example `4/5 participants`, rather than presenting a small study as population-level evidence.

Do not publish a resume impact claim until:

- At least five sessions are complete.
- Both baseline and assisted tasks are scored.
- The scoring method is documented.
- Any quoted feedback has explicit participant permission.

## Resume-claim template

Use only after real results exist:

> In five moderated tests, reduced median time to the first usable research question from [baseline] to [assisted], while [x/y] participants rated the output specific enough to test.
